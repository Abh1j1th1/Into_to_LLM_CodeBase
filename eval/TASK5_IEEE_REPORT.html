Failure-Aware Tool-Use Agents and Trajectory-Aware Evaluation on AgentBench FC

Group 12: Abhijith S Kumar (2024A7PS0557G), Joseph Sebastian (2024A8PS0681G), Rahul Sunil (2024A7PS0050G), D A Pavan Shenoy (2024A7PS0584G), Shreyash Singh (2024ADPS0860G)

Department of Computer Science & Information Systems, BITS Pilani K. K. Birla Goa Campus

Abstract—Evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) as autonomous tool-using agents requires multi-turn interaction in stateful environments, yet standard benchmarks rely on single greedy runs, coarse symptom-level failure buckets, and ad hoc reciprocal-weighted aggregation. In this work, we consolidate our end-to-end investigation of AgentBench Function Calling (FC) across dbbench-std and os-std. Guided by an Iteration 1 pilot (Qwen3-8B, N = 10/task) that revealed 9/10 OS failures stemmed from answering after a single unverified command rather than Task-Limit-Exceeded (TLE) loops, we evolved our design into a client-side failure-aware intervention suite (c0–c4) featuring a budget-aware targeted verify gate (c3) alongside a Trajectory-Aware Bayesian Item Response Theory (IRT) scoring framework. In Iteration 2, we evaluated local open-weight models via Ollama on a stratified 40-item subset (20 DB + 20 OS, T = 0.0). On qwen2.5:3b (80 episodes), c3 reliably enforced multi-step verification (reducing ≤1-command answers from 50.0% to 10.0%, gate fired on 67.5% of episodes) at ~2× prompt-token (4,241.4 → 8,403.9) and latency (16.30 s → 28.42 s) cost, without a statistically significant accuracy gain (27.5% → 25.0%, McNemar exact p = 1.0000). Larger local models (qwen2.5:7b at 28.9% c0 over 38 episodes; gemma4:12b at 50.0% DB c0 over 8 episodes) hit controller session-expiration timeouts under 6 GB VRAM constraints.

Index Terms—LLM agents, tool use, AgentBench FC, verification gate, failure memory, Bayesian IRT, local inference.

I. Introduction

A. Background Motivation and Problem Definition

The evaluation of Large Language Models (LLMs) has shifted from static, single-turn natural language tasks toward autonomous multi-turn agents capable of external tool invocation and environment interaction [1]. However, systematic gap analysis of AgentBench [1] reveals five structural limitations: (1) lack of statistical rigor, as greedy decoding (T = 0) with a single run per instance and uneven test splits (e.g., 20 Digital Card Game queries vs. 144 OS and 200 Web Shopping items) provides no confidence intervals or variance estimates; (2) coarse, confounded failure diagnosis, as Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting is evaluated alone with a five-bucket symptom taxonomy (CLE/IF/IA/TLE/Complete) and a single ROUGE-L repetition metric P(n, t) applied only to TLE cases; (3) unvalidated automated judges in Lateral Thinking Puzzles (Appendix F.2); (4) incommensurable metric aggregation, collapsing Success Rate, F1, Reward, and Game Progress via 2023-pool reciprocal weights into one Overall Score; and (5) unablated 3,500-token context truncation on long-horizon tasks.

Furthermore, our Iteration 1 pilot on AgentBench FC (Qwen3-8B) uncovered a critical behavioral shift: whereas AgentBench v0.2 reported TLE repetition loops (avg. 25.5 rounds, >90% with ROUGE-L ≥ 0.8) as the dominant failure, modern tool-calling 3B–8B models frequently commit an answer after ≤1 command without verifying tool outputs (9/10 OS pilot failures; 50.0% of qwen2.5:3b and 36.8% of qwen2.5:7b baseline episodes).

  3. Trajectory-Aware Bayesian IRT Framework: Implemented 1PL, 2PL, and trajectory-aware 2PL IRT models (eval/irt.py) with Gaussian priors and Laplace uncertainty, alongside temperature-filtered paired McNemar analysis (eval/analyze_proposal3.py).

II. Related Work

Following our automated literature retrieval and manual filtering (excluding unrelated physical-dynamics papers and off-topic multimodal synergy studies [5]), prior work organizes into three areas:

A. Interactive LLM Agent Benchmarks

AgentBench [1] evaluates 29 LLMs across eight interactive environments using text-prompted interaction and reciprocal-weighted scoring, later updated to the October 2025 Function Calling (FC) release on AgentRL. While AgentBench establishes capability gaps between commercial and open-weight models, its single-run greedy protocol motivates budget-efficient, statistically grounded re-evaluation.

B. Trajectory-Aware Evaluation and Item Response Theory

Recent work on Efficient SWE Agent Benchmarking introduces PTA-IRT [2], demonstrating that execution trajectories (search paths, intermediate diffs, and logs) serve as privileged information within an Item Response Theory framework to recover full-benchmark rankings from small calibration subsets (Kendall’s τ ≈ 0.89, Spearman’s ρ ≈ 0.97). We adapt this paradigm from single-domain SWE tasks to AgentBench’s multi-environment (DB + OS) setting.

C. Failure-Aware Agents, Self-Correction, and Automated Judges

Huang et al. [4] show that LLMs rarely self-correct reasoning from generic prompts without external feedback, explaining why our Iteration 1 generic verify prompt failed to flip any outcome and motivating our structured, independent-command verify gate (c3). Separately, mechanistic interpretability of LLM-as-a-Judge models (Themis, Prometheus) in “Beyond Scores” [3] reveals two-stage evaluation circuits (early-layer discrepancy routing and late-layer MLP integration), motivating our dropped Proposal 2 judge-auditing line.

III. Problem Formulation

A. Multi-Turn Episode with Client-Side Interventions

Each benchmark item i ∈ IDB ∪ IOS defines an interactive episode over turns t = 1, …, Renv with round budget RDB = 15 and ROS = 8. At turn t, given history ht, policy πθ emits tool calls at ∈ Tenv ∪ Tcommit (where Tenv = {execute_sql} or {bash_action}). A client-side operator Φc(ht, at, st) maintains episode state st (environment call count nenv, normalized action history, and failure records) without altering server reward ym,i ∈ {0, 1}:

Φc3 holds commit at ⇔ (not fired) ∧ (Renv − nenv ≥ 2)

When triggered, Φc3 injects a synthetic tool-hold response and requires the agent to map question constraints to observed outputs and execute a second independent Tenv command before re-committing.

B. Trajectory-Aware Bayesian IRT

Given an observed binary matrix Y ∈ {0, 1}M×I across M model-condition configurations and I items, Proposal 1 models pass probability via regularized MAP estimation with Gaussian priors and Laplace posterior standard errors:

P(ym,i = 1 | θm, ai, bi) = σ(ai(θm − bi))

where θm is latent ability, bi is item difficulty, ai > 0 is item discrimination (ai = 1 in 1PL), and traj_2pl jointly models auxiliary trajectory features (α0, α1). Proposal 2 (mechanistic judge auditing) and the negative-results literature pipeline from Assignment 2 were scoped out because DB/OS use deterministic checkers rather than LLM judges.

IV. Methodology

A. Dataset and Environments

Evaluations run live on Dockerized dbbench-std (MySQL containers, 300 items) and os-std (Ubuntu bash containers, 144 items) orchestrated by agentrl-controller and Redis. Under consumer 6 GB VRAM constraints, we evaluate on the stratified 40-item benchmark (eval/stratified_40.json: 20 DB across SQL types + 20 OS across directories 1–7) at greedy decoding (T = 0.0, concurrency 1, OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH=8192).

B. Failure-Aware Agent Conditions (c0–c4)

All conditions are client-side wrappers in eval/conditions.py: c0 (baseline), c1 (repeated-action warning for exact or ROUGE-L F1 ≥ 0.85 matches), c2 (state-conditioned failure memory for execution errors, empty non-mutating outputs, truncation, and repeats), c3 (targeted verify gate holding the first commit when ≥2 rounds remain), and c4 (c2 + c3).

C. Statistical Verification Protocol

eval/analyze_proposal3.py excludes any T > 0 rows via both row metadata and filename tags so c0 remains strictly greedy. Paired comparisons use exact two-sided McNemar’s tests on matched item IDs alongside behavioral diagnostics (≤1-command rate, TLE rate, mean environment calls, P(10, 0.8) repetition, token usage, and latency).

V. Experimentation, Results, and Discussion

A. Iteration 1 Pilot & Iteration 2 Local Setup

In Iteration 1 (Colab T4 + Intel Mac, Qwen3-8B 4-bit, first 10 indices/task), c0 scored 7/10 on DB and 0/10 on OS (9/10 OS episodes ended after 1 command); enabling thinking mode (1/9 OS) was 28× slower, and a generic c3 prompt (7/10 DB, 0/10 OS) never flipped an outcome. In Iteration 2, we evaluated three local Ollama models on Windows 11 (Ryzen 7 7840HS, 16 GB RAM, RTX 3050 6 GB): qwen2.5:3b (100% GPU, full c0 + c3, 80 episodes), qwen2.5:7b (21%/79% CPU/GPU, 38 c0 episodes), and gemma4:12b (8.0 GB on 6 GB VRAM, 8 DB c0 episodes).

B. Key Findings and Trade-Offs

First, baseline c0 agents frequently stop after ≤1 command (50.0% on qwen2.5:3b, 36.8% on qwen2.5:7b). Second, c3 reduced ≤1-command answers from 50.0% to 10.0% on qwen2.5:3b (gate fired on 67.5% of episodes) and from 10.0% to 0.0% on qwen2.5:7b DB, which is true by construction when the gate holds the first commit. Third, paired accuracy deltas were not statistically significant on either qwen2.5:3b (27.5% → 25.0%, +4/−5, McNemar p = 1.0000; DB p = 0.6250; OS p = 1.0000) or qwen2.5:7b DB (45.0% → 40.0%, +0/−1, p = 1.0000), while increasing prompt tokens (4,241.4 → 8,403.9 on 3B), latency (16.30 s → 28.42 s on 3B), and P(10, 0.8) repetition (50.0% → 75.0% on 3B; 85.0% → 100.0% on 7B DB).

C. Local Hardware Bottlenecks & 2-Round Refinement Log

qwen2.5:7b completed 20/20 DB (45.0%) and 18/20 OS (11.1%) before stopping when os-std index 76 hung twice (session 127 and 129 expired). gemma4:12b completed 8/20 DB (50.0%) before stalling past controller timeouts on indices 67 (session 138 expired) and 93 (session 139 stalled >10 min even with thinking_off: true). Proposal 1 (analyze_proposal1.py) was skipped because only qwen2.5:3b has paired c0/c3 coverage; moreover, Proposal 1’s subset selection is fit on the same matrix it is scored against, and 5-fold CV over (model, item) cells leaks same-item/other-condition outcomes.

VI. Conclusion

Client-side failure-aware interventions integrate cleanly into AgentBench FC without modifying task grading. On qwen2.5:3b (N = 40), the c3 verify gate reliably enforces multi-step verification (≤1-command rate 50.0% → 10.0%) at ~2× token and latency cost without a statistically significant accuracy gain (p = 1.0000). Evaluating 7B–12B models on 6 GB VRAM hardware is bottlenecked by controller session timeouts, motivating higher-VRAM multi-model runs to evaluate c1/c2/c4 and Proposal 1 IRT calibration.
